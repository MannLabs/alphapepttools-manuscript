\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
% 500 words
Molecular profiling by MS-based proteomics enables the characterization of biological and clinical samples at high-throughput and increasing depth.
MS-based proteomics is now routinely applied in both basic research and clinical settings to identify critical regulators of disease, contributing to a deeper understanding of the biology of diverse human conditions \cite{Nordmann.2024,Rosenberger.2025}. \smallskip

The technology further advances with respect to proteomic depth \cite{Rosenberger.2023}, scale \cite{Albrecht.2025,Niu.2025}, and integration with additional modalities \cite{Fulcher.2024,Mund.2022,Zheng.2025a}.
In line with developments in adjacent -omics fields such as RNA sequencing, MS-based proteomics is now applicable from highly resolved low-input and single-cell experiments \cite{Rosenberger.2023,Slavov.2026,Gatto.2023}, to spatial contexts \cite{NatureMethods.2024}, and large clinical cohorts including plasma proteomics \cite{Bader.2023}. This creates increasing demands for scalable, interoperable analysis frameworks.
\smallskip

Extracting biological insights from this wealth of information is the central goal of downstream analysis.
Scalable analysis across modalities and experiments requires the integration of diverse computational tools including search engines, downstream analysis packages, and specialized software for biological interpretation.
However, the heterogeneous output formats of current mass spectrometry workflows increase the overhead of connecting these tools.
The limited adoption of common standards or best practices in downstream analysis complicates the performant, reproducible data analysis.
These challenges affect not only computational scientists developing generalizable tools but also non-computational domain experts, such as clinicians, who need to focus on the biological questions at hand.
\smallskip

Several software solutions address parts of this problem.
GUI-based platforms such as Perseus \cite{Tyanova.2018} provide accessible interfaces for statistical analysis but do not scale readily to large cohorts or integrate natively with multi-omics workflows.
Dedicated R packages, including MSstats \cite{Choi.2014,Kohler.2023} and DEP \cite{Zhang.2018a}, %no longer supported(End of life on bioconductor)
offer robust statistical frameworks for specific tasks but operate within a fragmented ecosystem with limited interoperability as they do not build on shared data structures that are interoperable with domain-specific packages for the biological interpretation of the data.
\smallskip

Parallel challenges during the scaling of single-cell RNA-sequencing datasets in recent years, have spurred the development of widely adopted software ecosystems such as Bioconductor \cite{Amezquita.2020} in R and the scverse project \cite{Virshup.2023} in Python.
Especially the anndata \cite{Virshup.2021}, a data container that aligns experimental metadata with the experimental measurement in a single, wide-formatted data structure, in scRNAseq common data structures at the core of the scverse \cite{Virshup.2023} ecosystems have greatly improved interoperability and modularity across methods has sparked a rich open source community and has been shown to be applicable to -omics modalities \cite{Bredikhin.2022,Marconato.2024,Heumos.2024}.
While some software solutions in this ecosystem such as scanpy \cite{Wolf.2018} for the analysis of scRNAseq data, sceptre \cite{Schoof.2021}, and msmu \cite{Choi.2026} are in parts applicable to proteomics data and have demonstrated the potential of shared data structures \cite{Furtwangler.2025}, they do not provide an end-to-end solution for the analysis of proteomics data, such as accounting for differences in data distribution, impact of missing values, and hierarchical feature organization intrinsic to bottom up proteomics. \smallskip

Taking the scverse ecosystem as precedence, we sought to build that analogous structures for proteomics.
Here, we present alphapepttools: a performant, scalable, and extensible computational framework for the analysis of modern proteomics data built on the anndata standard.
We showcase that alphapepttools can analyze proteomics data from various flavors of MS-based proteomics, including body fluids, peptidomics, single cells, and bulk samples, independent of the search and quantification engine used.
We further demonstrate its ability to integrate proteomics data with other omics modalities, including spatial information and RNA-sequencing data.
By building on the anndata standard, alphapepttools is further fully compatible with a range of domain-specific analysis packages.

More broadly, we hope to catalyze the development of novel, widely applicable analysis methods that bridge proteomics data analyses with adjacent omics modalities and computational ecosystems.
